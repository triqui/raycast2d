09 - Thin walls and one-way surfaces

Drag the ring to reposition the source. The cross marks the destination and follows the pointer.

Thin is geometry, one-way is a rule, and the two are independent. All four combinations are on the map: a thick wall and a thin rail, each in a two-way and a one-way version.

  • Grey obstacles are tiles, pink ones are loose rectangles.
  • The hairs mark the side a one-way surface stops.
  • Thickness is chosen when the obstacle is built; the direction it stops from is chosen by the filter, at the moment of the cast.

addRectangle(x, y, width, 0, userData)
A rectangle of zero height blocks along a line rather than across a cell.

  • A ray running exactly along it meets its end rather than passing by, since a rectangle of no height still has two short sides.
  • A ray starting exactly on it meets it at distance zero with startedInside false, because there is no inside to be in, so ignoreInside will not skip it.

The direction is on the data as a vector, and the filter is a dot product: normalX * way.x + normalY * way.y > 0.7 means stop only on the face whose normal is this. The same line works on a tile, on a rail and on a vertical door.

On the grey one-way tile the bright edge suggests a line, but the whole cell blocks. Come at it from the side and the ray stops at the cell boundary, a whole tile before the edge that is drawn.
